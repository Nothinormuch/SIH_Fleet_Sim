BIOS Engineering Rationale and Evidence Boundaries
SIH26123 · Conditional design arguments, not a universal correctness proof
Scope and current status

This document retains its historical filename for existing links. It replaces unsupported mathematical and percentage claims with the implemented mechanisms, their assumptions and reproducible evidence. BIOS 7 is a candidate under verification, not a released default.

The frozen 5cce5de fixed-floor, 50-AMR, seed-0 test ended at 1,200 simulated seconds with 99/100 tasks for both current-source BIOS 6 and BIOS 7, and zero observed robot–robot, robot–human and robot–rack contacts. That is a liveness failure, not a release pass. Later cache, charger and idle-tail changes require a fresh passing campaign; no pending result is assumed successful. See the retained raw regression result and release checklist.

Separate allocation, traffic and protective stopping

Task allocation chooses work; traffic coordination negotiates shared space; local stopping reacts to sensor input. The stop-and-wait reference can use the same task allocator as BIOS, so it must not be described as necessarily lacking communication, energy checks or multiple tasks. It waits when movement is blocked and may resume when space clears. Persistent circular waits are possible; not every stop is permanent.

Centralized references are separate comparisons. A failed reference implementation is not evidence that every centralized planner or industrial fleet system is inferior. The repository's known reference limitations are recorded in the BIOS 7 experiment notes.

Why the mechanisms may help
  • Traffic priorities and expiring leases: deterministic tie-breaking, bounded reservations and local recovery help resolve competing movement requests. Lease expiry removes stale software authority; it does not remove a failed robot's physical body or guarantee an available escape route.
  • Energy-aware allocation: estimates include approach, cargo-dependent travel and handling, and return to a configured reachable charger. Current battery reserve and hard deadlines are checked against the estimate. This is conditional on the model and inputs, not proof that real battery consumption or completion time cannot exceed the estimate. Legacy maps without docks do not prove return-to-dock feasibility.
  • BIOS 6 and Auction V2: event-triggered messages, congestion experience and bounded future reservations aim to reduce waiting and redundant communication. A bounded future slot does not bound all candidate-evaluation work: opening an auction can still inspect the available task catalog.
  • BIOS 7 candidate: observed completion of a loaded corridor passage can release that task's auction-admission pressure. Physical occupancy, movement leases and local stopping remain authoritative. Stale or missing observations retain the conservative behavior.
Conditional assumptions, not universal guarantees

Useful progress requires a feasible task catalog, reachable destinations, sufficient energy and service capacity, room for safe yielding, sufficiently accurate localization and sensing, functioning command/watchdog paths, and the communication conditions required by the selected protocol. Permanent obstruction, saturation, stale observations or failures can prevent completion. Graph-level reasoning does not by itself establish safety for continuous chassis motion.

No proof here establishes deadlock freedom for every BIOS variant, a fixed complexity for time-to-deadlock, logarithmic or linear fleet speedup, or a formula linking prediction accuracy directly to collision probability. More communication can add congestion and computation; prediction can be wrong. These tradeoffs require measurement.

The Fischer–Lynch–Paterson result concerns the impossibility of guaranteed termination of deterministic consensus in every fully asynchronous execution with a possible crash, even with reliable messages. It does not prohibit agreement in all executions or validate this robot implementation.

How evidence must be read

The historical three-seed, ten-AMR development comparison used candidate snapshot 12d1ac2 and untouched control 8e4ab51. Each BIOS policy completed 90 tasks across three runs with zero observed contacts. These are finite historical observations, not current-release approval or 90 independent runs. Different campaign sizes must not be converted into a success-rate or speedup gain.

For two safely completed runs with identical inputs, completion-time reduction is 100 × (1 − candidate time / baseline time). If the baseline reaches a simulation cutoff unfinished, report only a conservative lower bound using its observed window, not an exact completion time. An external worker error or resource timeout provides no performance score. Contact-free prefixes do not establish future safety.

Report completion, all contact categories, messages, bytes, compute cost, scenario coverage and determinism separately. Shared-host headless timings are not unloaded live-control measurements or Raspberry Pi measurements. Preserve failed and unrun cases. The earlier capacity screen also retained its 50-AMR rack-contact failure rather than advertising a scalability guarantee.

What must pass before promotion

The release checklist requires the fixed failed-case retest, broader registered fleets and workloads, SIH overlap comparisons, measured human/fault interactions, strict same-source V6 nonregression, semantic repeats and independent-controller evidence. Previously observed seeds must be identified as retests. Improvements in one metric cannot hide a failed safety or completion gate.

BIOS's local stop logic is software, not a certified protective device. A real AMR needs a supported driver, validated sensing/localization and braking behavior, commissioning, and its independent manufacturer/integrator safety chain. No physical safety certification, universal hardware compatibility or universal task-completion claim is made.
